# 1. Domain Aggregate Roots

In a modern B2B CRM based on Domain-Driven Design (DDD), Aggregate Roots (AR) are the main entities that ensure data consistency, set transactional boundaries, and expose business behavior to the application layers.

Below are the key Aggregate Roots defined for this domain.

## 1. Company (Account)
- **What it represents:** A B2B company or organization with which there is or is expected to be a business relationship. In B2B, the *company* is the center of operations, not the individual person.
- **Why it is an aggregate root:** It encapsulates the entire corporate identity and groups all contacts (employees) who work in it, as well as their addresses. Modifying a contact or adding a new one is always done through the context of the company to guarantee rules (e.g., not exceeding limits, ensuring a primary contact).
- **Invariants it protects:**
  - Must have a trade name and, optionally, a tax identifier or web domain (e.g., `acme.com`) that is usually unique per Tenant.
  - There cannot be two identical contacts within the same company (same email).
- **Business operations:**
  - `RegisterCompany()`
  - `UpdateCompanyProfile()`
  - `AddContact()`
  - `RemoveContact()`
  - `DesignatePrimaryContact()`

## 2. Lead
- **What it represents:** A commercial interest in its earliest phase, generated by inbound campaigns or outbound actions. It is not yet qualified, nor has a formal sales relationship been established.
- **Why it is an aggregate root:** It has a short-lived life cycle and is completely independent of the rest of the entities. A Lead enters, is worked on, and at the end of the process is *converted* (giving rise to a `Company`, a `Contact`, and a `Deal`) or is *discarded*.
- **Invariants it protects:**
  - A Lead must contain a minimum valid means of contact (an email, a phone number, etc.).
  - A Lead in `Converted` or `Disqualified` state cannot go back to `New` state without a formal re-evaluation.
- **Business operations:**
  - `CaptureLead()`
  - `QualifyLead()`
  - `DisqualifyLead()`
  - `Convert()`

## 3. Deal (Opportunity)
- **What it represents:** A possible sale or specific business being managed with a `Company`.
- **Why it is an aggregate root:** The sales pipeline revolves around Deals. Each Deal advances independently through the sales stages. Modifying a Deal (changing its value, changing its stage) does not require blocking the `Company` entity.
- **Invariants it protects:**
  - Every Deal must be associated with a `Company` ID.
  - The economic amount or expected value (`EstimatedValue`) can never be negative.
  - A stage transition (e.g., from *Discovery* to *Proposal*) must follow the flow allowed by the Pipeline, and cannot skip states if business rules do not allow it, or if requirements are missing (e.g., sending a proposal requires an attached document).
- **Business operations:**
  - `OpenDeal()`
  - `ChangeStage()`
  - `UpdateEstimatedValue()`
  - `MarkAsWon()`
  - `MarkAsLost()`

## 4. Activity (Task / Interaction)
- **What it represents:** Any action registered by a sales user: a call, an email, a meeting, or a pending task related to a Lead, a Company, or a Deal.
- **Why it is an aggregate root:** Although conceptually it belongs to a client or a business, technically it is better to treat it as its own AR (which references the others by ID). This avoids concurrency problems: if two salespeople add notes to the same Deal at the same time, the Deal should not be blocked. In addition, activities have their own life cycle (they can be reassigned to an owner, postponed, or completed in isolation).
- **Invariants it protects:**
  - Every activity must obligatorily reference a main entity (`TargetId` and `TargetType` - e.g., a Deal or a Lead) and an owner (`OwnerId`).
  - If the activity type is `Meeting`, it must have consistent start and end dates (start <= end).
- **Business operations:**
  - `ScheduleActivity()`
  - `CompleteActivity()`
  - `RescheduleActivity()`
  - `ReassignActivity()`

## 5. User (Sales Representative)
- **What it represents:** An employee who operates the CRM, such as an Account Executive, an SDR (Sales Development Representative), or a Manager.
- **Why it is an aggregate root:** It controls access, team membership, roles, and the assignment of use licenses.
- **Invariants it protects:**
  - The access email must be unique per installation/tenant.
  - Must have at least one assigned role to operate.
- **Business operations:**
  - `RegisterUser()`
  - `AssignRole()`
  - `DeactivateUser()`

## 6. Tenant - *If multitenancy applies*
- **What it represents:** The workspace or client organization that has contracted our CRM software.
- **Why it is an aggregate root:** It manages the global configuration limits of the system (subscription, user limits, base Pipeline configuration).
- **Invariants it protects:**
  - An active Tenant allows access to its users; if suspended, access is blocked.
- **Business operations:**
  - `ProvisionTenant()`
  - `UpdateSubscription()`
  - `SuspendTenant()`
